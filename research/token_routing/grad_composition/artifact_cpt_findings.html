Prasann asked why token dropping keeps losing in CPT. Setup: 48 dev rows of 32,768 tokens eval_size = 48 rows, scored on the CPT start (stage 2), dense CPT after 12–480 steps (LR 2e-5), the online router OTR10M7 after 310 steps, and OLMo's own stage 3. A second backward with every attention layer's keys and values detached separates each token's own-label gradient from what it receives for serving as context.

  • Importance is flat after CPT, so a 10% keep cannot hold the gradient. After 12 or more dense steps the top 10% of tokens hold 0.62 of block 24's gradient energy and the top 30% hold 0.90 (SFT: 0.90+ in the top 10%). A per-row top 10% by G7 holds 0.37 of G24² (random 0.11), and the 50/10/3 pyramid holds 0.29. The rankings themselves are stable (ρ(G7, G24) = 0.66, higher than SFT's 0.34). It is the budget that fails: any routed keep needs about 30%.
  • Early blocks learn from serving as context; late blocks learn from their own label. The context share of a token's gradient is 0.95 at block 0, 0.80 at block 8, 0.42 at 16 and 0.21 at 24 after CPT, crossing 0.5 at about block 14. Dropping a token from block 7 on also removes 0.6–0.8 of the gradient its early-mid blocks would get as context. KV-only dropping from about block 20 on is the cut CPT tolerates best (context share ≤ 0.27 there).
  • The early stack's parameter-gradient share drops in the first 12 steps and then stays put. Blocks 0–6 hold 0.42 → 0.20 → 0.16 (base, 12 steps, 480 steps). In the base a few recurring tokens dominate the gradient (the top 1% hold 0.74–0.80) and norm parameters take 0.29 of it. Twelve dense steps remove both, and the LM head's share triples (0.11 → 0.30). OLMo's stage 3 keeps the base pattern. Freezing blocks 0–6 after warmup gives up about 1/6 of the gradient energy.
  • The dev-CE gain is mostly local copying, not long context. Dense 480 gains +0.169 in the first 4k and +0.167 at 16–32k. Of the gain at positions ≥ 4k, 56–66% comes from targets that repeat a 4-gram seen within the last 4,096 tokens (22.5% of targets, +0.35 to +0.41 each). Only 6–7% needs reach beyond every sliding window (3.3% of targets).
  • The CPT router checkpoint looks like dense on every statistic, loss 1.645 vs 1.620. Unlike the SFT router, it shows no adaptation to its own drop.
