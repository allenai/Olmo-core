Token-dropping continued pretraining on OLMo 3 7B: an empirical screen

Starting from a 7B base that has only ever seen 8k contexts, does dropping tokens during long-context continued pretraining reach dense's dev loss for less compute, at every budget, not only the smallest?

screen done  The CPT recipe keeps every token and trains no full-rank weights: freeze the whole model, LM head included, and train rank-64 adapters on all 32 blocks. It costs 0.685× dense FLOPs and 22.3 GiB peak (dense 35.2). At 32k it is never behind dense at the same step count up to 960 steps; its FLOPs-to-match m runs 4.8 / 4.3 / 3.2 / 2.1 / ~1.4 at 48 / 120 / 240 / 480 / 960 steps. The edge is larger at long context: −0.0057 at 64k and −0.0046 at 128k after 120 steps (full-row CE). Every method that drops tokens, depth or MLP width loses.

What loses. Every arm that drops tokens: random-block and first+last input drops, sd20-style slots, the CTC suite's mid-stack online router and all its cuts, and mixture-of-depths. All-local windows and warmup-then-freeze lose too. Dense saturates in about 12 steps, and its gain is mostly local copying (56–66% of the gain beyond 4k comes from 4-grams repeated within the last 4k), so a label that skips depth costs more than the FLOPs it saves.

What wins: LoRA in place of full fine-tuning. The embeddings and the first K blocks are frozen from step 0 and get rank-64 adapters (scale 32); later blocks train fully. Numbers are on dev32k (1,024 rows), FLOPs from the meter, paired against dense at the same step count:

  • Each step deeper is cheaper and better. Paired SE on every delta is ±0.0001. Seed noise on overall CE is ±0.0001 (K16, seed 1: 1.6835 vs 1.6834).
  • Long-range bin (targets whose 4-gram last appeared more than 4k back): seeds differ by 0.0023, so LoRA's ≤0.002 gaps there are not resolved.
  • K32 uses long context as well as dense does: its context gain (CE with an 8k window minus CE with full 32k context, on each row's last 4k) is 0.0681 / 0.0700 / 0.0725 / 0.0739 at 12 / 24 / 48 / 120 steps, against dense's 0.0671 / 0.0700 / 0.0726 / 0.0750 (± 0.0036).
  • K32 reproduces: seed 1 at 120 steps gives 1.6821 against seed 0's 1.6819.
  • Rank 128 vs 64 at K16 (same scale): no better. Higher adapter LR hurts K32: LR 4e-5 gives 1.6843 at 120 steps, and rank 128 at scale 64 gives 1.6874. The scale multiplies the adapter update directly, so scale 64 doubles the adapters' effective LR, and that run says nothing about rank. LR 1e-5 ties 2e-5 (1.6820 vs 1.6819 at 120 steps), with slightly less forgetting on the short guard (1.1575 vs 1.1607; 256 rows).
  • The edge decays with budget: K24 is −0.0023 / −0.0026 / −0.0016 / −0.0009 at 48 / 120 / 240 / 480 steps, so its m falls from ~2.8 to 1.9 at 240 and to just above 1.3 at 480 (a lower bound there, past the last dense point). At large budgets most of the gain is the cheaper step, not a better CE. K32 decays more slowly: −0.0031 / −0.0034 / −0.0023 at 48 / 120 / 240 steps (1.6804 at 240 for 4,711 PF; m 3.9 / 3.45 / 2.5). K32 at 480 is running.
  • Stacking a half-width MLP on K32 fails badly: 0.534× FLOPs/step but 1.7700 at 48 steps, +0.082 worse than dense and worse than dense after only 4 steps. Training uses a random 50% of MLP neurons each step with a 1/f rescale, and eval runs full width. With the base frozen, the adapters cannot absorb that much dropout. It gets worse with training (1.7796 at 120 steps), and the quarter-width runs were cancelled. Random 75% width scores 1.7124. Keeping the most active neurons instead (top-k, no rescale) recovers about a third of the loss: 1.7342 at 50% width and 1.6999 at 75%, still worse than dense at 8 steps (m ≈ 0.3 at best). Letting the base train does not help either. Dense partial MLP at 50% / 25% width gets worse with training: 1.7402 → 1.7647 and 1.7480 → 1.8663 from 24 to 120 steps. Partial MLP is closed for this CPT.
  • Stopping backprop below block B loses too. These runs freeze the bottom B blocks with no adapters and put LoRA only above them. At 120 steps, B = 4 gives 1.6828 and B = 8 gives 1.6857, against K32's 1.6819. At 48 steps, B = 16 gives 1.7002. Every block taken out of training costs more quality than its backward pass saves.
  • Token dropping still loses on top of K32: a router keeping 50% of tokens after block 7 (0.412× FLOPs/step) scores 1.7247 / 1.7120 at 48 / 120 steps. Both are worse than dense after just 4 steps (m < 0.24).
  • Smaller batches do not help K32: 8 rows/step at the same tokens (96 optimizer steps) gives 1.6846 against 1.6849 at 16 rows/step, a tie.
  • The win is larger at long context. These are full-row numbers; an earlier version scored only the first 32k targets of each row. At 64k (512 dev rows) K32 is −0.0050 / −0.0030 / −0.0017 ± 0.0001–0.0002 against dense at 120 / 240 / 480 steps, about 1.4–1.5× the 32k gap, and the same on the 32k–64k positions. At 128k it is −0.0035 / −0.0041 at 48 / 120 steps: about the same size as at 64k, not growing further.

m is read off the measured dense curve, which now runs to 960 steps (1.6762 at 26,349 PF). The meter does not charge activation-checkpoint recompute, so K32 is ≈ 0.75× dense in real FLOPs; every m here is about 7% lower on that basis.

How big is the win? m peaks at about 3.9× around 48–120 steps, decays to about 1.8× at 480 steps, and reaches 1.1× at 960 steps. There K32 is 0.0012 ± 0.0001 behind dense at the same step count, and 0.0035 behind on long-range targets: the adapters saturate while full CPT keeps learning. m is larger at 64k. That is real, but short of the 10–20× target. At large budgets most of the value is the 0.70× step and 29% less memory, not a better optimum. Every per-step cut stacked on K32 lost: partial MLP (random and top-k), a token router keeping 50%, 8 rows/step, and an adapter-less frozen bottom stack.

Running now (Beaker): K32 at 240 / 480 steps, a second seed, rank 128, LR 1e-5, and 64k context; K30 at 120, K28 at 240, K24 at 480, K16 at 64k. Balrog A100s: partial-MLP widths ± LoRA.

Max trainable context per GPU

Memory probe on 8 × H200: FSDP, one row per GPU, full activation checkpointing, fused-linear CE, three real steps on synthetic joined rows. Peak active memory:

armGiB per +32k tokensmax T, 80 GB-classmax T, H200
dense20.1~95k~190k
K32 (whole-model LoRA)20.3~111k~206k
dense + offload12.7~126k~277k
K32 + offload11.6~157k~323k

LoRA removes ~12 GiB of fixed state (grads and Adam state of frozen weights) but leaves the per-token slope unchanged, so on its own it adds only 8–17% to max T. The slope is the stored block inputs, and offload takes most of it off the GPU: +46% max T for dense and +70% with K32. Offload is an implementation fix, not a method win. FLOPs and wall-clock also diverge at this length: K32 is 0.70× dense FLOPs but only ~3% faster per token at 128–192k, where attention dominates.

Why this experiment

Earlier token-drop CPT ran on Qwen3.5-4B, which had already been through long-context training. Soft-detached pooling (sd20) beat dense about 4× at small budgets, then lost near 2k PF; the deficit sat at long-range positions and grew with budget. OLMo 3 publishes its stage-2 checkpoint, the point right before its own long-context stage, so long-range learning is what this CPT has to do.

Known limits

  • Dropping tokens breaks OLMo 3's sliding windows unless masked by original position (step 4).
  • Dev CE only so far; generation-graded CTC evals come with the confirm round.
  • 64k FLOP ratios for the drop arms are estimates until their meters report.
  • The forgetting guard comes from OLMo stage-3 training data, so it is a relative check across arms.
  • Long-range (far-repeat) CE varies by ~0.002 between seeds; deltas that small are not resolved.
  • LoRA gains are measured on dev CE only; no generation-graded long-context eval yet.
