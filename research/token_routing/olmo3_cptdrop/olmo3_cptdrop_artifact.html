Token-dropping continued pretraining on OLMo 3 7B: an empirical screen

Starting from a 7B base that has only ever seen 8k contexts, does dropping tokens during long-context continued pretraining reach dense's dev loss for less compute, at every budget, not only the smallest?

in progress  A LoRA early stack beats dense: at rank 64 on blocks 0–15 it has lower dev CE than dense at every budget, with 15% fewer FLOPs and 12% less peak GPU memory; on blocks 0–7 it matches dense even on long-range targets.

Everything that drops tokens loses in this CPT: random-block and first+last input drops, sd20-style slots, the CTC suite's mid-stack online router and its cuts (forward-only early stack, pyramid, merged summary tokens, early-exit loss, 50%-keep periodic steps), and mixture-of-depths at 50% and 25% capacity. Dense saturates within about 12 steps, and its gain is mostly local copying (grad-composition: 56–66% of the gain beyond 4k comes from 4-grams repeated within the last 4k). Every label must go through the full depth. The arms that keep labels intact do better. Freezing the embeddings and blocks 0–15 from step 0 and training rank-16 adapters there instead (scale 32) reaches CE 1.6841 at 2.8k PF against dense's 1.6853 at 3.3k PF (paired −0.0013 ± 0.0001, 1,024 rows), and 1.6879 vs 1.6880 at 48 steps. Peak memory is 30.8 GiB per GPU for the whole run, against 35.2 GiB. The multiplier reads 1.2–1.65× because the dense curve is flat there; the CE gain itself is about 0.001 nats. On targets that repeat text seen only more than 4k tokens back (1.25M targets, the long-range part of the gain), LoRA is worse than dense at every budget: +0.0066 / +0.0067 / +0.0040 nats at 12 / 48 / 120 steps (paired SE ±0.0002–0.0004), about 0.6× on that metric. Its overall edge comes from local and novel tokens. Training every layer with a 4096 window (all-local) loses badly: context gain falls to 0.044 and keeps falling. Warmup-then-freeze ties dense only at 120 steps and keeps the 35.9 GiB pre-freeze peak. Rank 64 fixes most of that. On blocks 0–15 (0.85× dense FLOPs, peak 30.9 GiB) it beats dense on overall CE by 0.0015–0.0030 nats at 12 / 24 / 48 / 120 steps, and its long-range deficit shrinks to +0.0012–0.0015 (from rank 16's +0.004–0.007). On blocks 0–7 (0.92×, 33.1 GiB) it is level with dense on long-range targets and slightly better overall. The peak saving is the frozen parameters' gradients and optimizer state; activations are unchanged. Running now: rank 64 at 240 / 480 steps (to read FLOPs-to-match-dense at normal budgets), rank 128, a second seed, and rank 64 frozen at 75% of the steps.

Short docs: 256 × 4k rows ⚠ eval_size=256 rows, a relative forgetting guard. The OLMo recipe LR (2.07e-4) plateaued at 1.82 and forgot (short docs up to 1.49); the probe that set 2e-5 is in STATUS.md.

Why this experiment

Earlier token-drop CPT ran on Qwen3.5-4B, which had already been through long-context training. Soft-detached pooling (sd20) beat dense about 4× at small budgets, then lost near 2k PF; the deficit sat at long-range positions and grew with budget. OLMo 3 publishes its stage-2 checkpoint, the point right before its own long-context stage, so long-range learning is what this CPT has to do.

Known limits

  • Dropping tokens breaks OLMo 3's sliding windows unless masked by original position (step 4).
  • Dev CE only so far; generation-graded CTC evals come with the confirm round.
  • 64k FLOP ratios for the drop arms are estimates until their meters report.
  • The forgetting guard comes from OLMo stage-3 training data, so it is a relative check across arms.
