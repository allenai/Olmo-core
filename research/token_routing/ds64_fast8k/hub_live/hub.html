Cheaper training that matches dense at matched FLOPs

Qwen3.5-4B SFT on CTC long-context tasks (4-task and 11-task mixes, 64k) and OLMo 3 7B long-document continued pretraining. Every plot puts training FLOPs (measured by the FLOP meter; PF = petaFLOPs) on the x-axis; up-and-left of the dense line is better (down-and-left for CPT loss). Private page.

1 · Summary

Where things stand

Routing plus LoRA trains ≈2–4× cheaper than dense on SFT at matched quality; in CPT, LoRA alone wins and every token drop loses.

Verdicts

  • SFT, 4-task mix: dense needs about 3× the training FLOPs to match the cheap methods on fresh data (3,904 rows/task), and ≈2× at 7,808 rows/task (now a point estimate: the recipe's 0.926 equals dense at 3,904 rows; dense gains only +0.005 from 3,904 to 7,808, so the 7,808 ratios are good only to ≈1.5–4×). On the original pool, dense needs ≈3.5–3.9× to match the recipe and ≈4.4× for whole-model LoRA + two-stage keep.
  • Late keep-widening (10-31), corrected after a second seed: at 7,808 rows/task the 2-seed mean is f1 0.931 vs the recipe's 0.926 (+0.005 ± ≈0.003) and answer CE 0.1404 vs 0.1439 (−0.0035 ± ≈0.004) for 8.6% more FLOPs: within seed noise, and still short of dense on CE (0.1374). The seed-0 result (>4.1×) was a lucky draw. Seed-to-seed spread at this scale is as large as every effect measured, so claims here need ≥3 seeds per arm.
  • SFT, 11-task mix: ≈1.7× for the recipe; whole-model LoRA + two-stage scores 0.762 against dense's 0.697 at the same 232 PF (2 seeds), ≈2.4×. Gains concentrate on xabsence, contradiction and outlier; four retrieval tasks are near dense's ceiling; grouping is the one task the router loses.
  • SFT, 11-task mix at 64k with 10× the data: the router (keep 10% after block 7, LR 5e-6) is ≈+0.03 task-average over dense at matched FLOPs at every budget from 1/4 to 1 epoch; dense needs ≈1.6–1.7× the FLOPs to match. On the same data it trails dense by ≈0.03. The recipe at 64k (1/4 epoch, one seed): +0.037 task-average at 0.365× dense FLOPs, ahead on all 10 graded tasks, 24% less peak active memory (37.2 vs 48.7 GiB); its gains grow with length (contradiction +0.041 at 8k → +0.119 at 64k).
  • The recipe: rank-64 LoRA adapters on blocks 0–6 (frozen base), an online gradient-norm router that keeps 20% of tokens from block 7 and rescores to 7% at block 16, no full-context steps, LR 5e-6, plus a 16-token floor per document on the 11-task mix. Whole-model LoRA (all 32 blocks, LR 1e-5) is cheaper still at the same quality.
  • LoRA vs routing (2×2 ablation): routing does most of the matched-FLOP gain; LoRA-all is a smaller add-on. Dense FLOPs to match, 4-task (original 1,464 / fresh 1,952 / fresh 3,904 rows per task): dense + LoRA-all 1.48 / 1.41 / 1.60×, routing alone 3.45 / 2.85 / 2.89×, both 4.82 / 4.63 / 3.81×. 11-task (240 / 480 / 720 rows per task): 1.39 / 1.15 / 1.06×, 1.79 / 1.67 / 1.56×, 1.83 / 2.09 / 2.30×. LoRA alone is 15–35% of the log multiplier on the 4-task mix; on the 11-task mix its share shrinks with data (54% → 19% → 7%). At matched data all four arms score within ≈0.03, so the gain is cheaper steps, not better learning. Dense is at its best LR throughout. A random token choice nearly matches the router (+0.027 at 1,464 rows, a tie at 3,904), so most of the routing gain comes from the drop structure. ⚠ Mostly 1–3 seeds.
  • CPT: every token-drop variant loses. Whole-model LoRA with no dropping beats dense at 0.70× metered FLOPs per step (≈0.75× counting checkpoint recompute): ≈2–4× FLOPs-to-match between 24 and 240 steps, ≈1.8× at 480, and parity by 960 steps (dev CE +0.0012 behind dense; ≈1.04× counting recompute). The adapters saturate around 0.25–0.5B tokens while full CPT keeps learning long-range structure, so this is a short-budget recipe. At 64k context the lead is ≈1.4–1.5× the 32k one, and about the same at 128k (frozen-output-layer recipe: −0.0057 / −0.0039 / −0.0026 at 64k after 120 / 240 / 480 steps, −0.0038 / −0.0046 at 128k after 48 / 120; the 64k 240-step run ties dense at 480, ≈2.9×). Freezing the output layer too is the CPT recipe (no full-rank weights trained at all; 0.685× FLOPs per step, 22.3 vs 35.2 GiB peak): never behind dense up to 960 steps. Dense FLOPs to match: 4.8× / 4.3× / 3.2× / 2.1× / ≈1.4× at 48 / 120 / 240 / 480 / 960 steps (dev CE −0.0037 / −0.0041 / −0.0032 / −0.0022 / +0.0002); seed 1 reproduces at 120. It also forgets less on short documents.
  • Max train length at 80 GB (SFT): ≈1.8× dense for the recipe and ≈2.4× for whole-model LoRA (extrapolated from short probes).

    Why it works where it works

    • Blocks 0–6 must see every token; the router decides at block 7 from features that predict each token's gradient norm (held-out Spearman 0.54–0.63). The gradient-composition study shows rankings drift with depth (Spearman between blocks 7 and 24 falls 0.57 → 0.34), which is why rescoring at block 16 helps. ⚠ probes on 32–64 rows/task.
    • In SFT only answer tokens carry loss, and the gradient concentrates on few document tokens (top 10% hold ≈0.9 of late-layer gradient energy).
    • In CPT every token is a label and importance is flat (top 10% hold 0.62); early blocks learn mostly as context for later tokens (0.95 of block-0 gradient). So dropping tokens removes most of the learning signal, while freezing weights behind adapters does not. ⚠ 48 rows × 32k.

The router recovers to >0.8 by step 31 (≈6% of training) and ends at 0.88 with norm ratio 1.00 by step 244, matching the fully trained checkpoint. Random never recovers (0.13–0.35; norm ratio grows to 6×). The aggressive budgets a ≥5× method would need (router keeping 10% after block 7, or 3% after block 16) touch 0.70–0.78 around step 31 and then decay to 0.38–0.39 on this recipe-trained model. A run trained at those budgets is the direct test. Because the biased phase is short, it is unlikely to explain most of the gap to the attribution bound on its own.

    Verdict. (1) Gradient attribution under the dense forward is not a proxy for routing: on the dense checkpoint, oracle attribution reaches 0.999 per step / 0.996 expected, but the true routed gradient at the same selection only 0.12 expected (router 0.04, random 0.03); >97% of the loss is the kept tokens' gradients shifting when context leaves the forward. (2) Routed training fixes this for the selection the model co-adapted with: on the recipe checkpoint the router-routed gradient is nearly unbiased (expected cosine 0.874 raw / 0.831 Adam, norm ratio 0.89 vs 8.3 on the dense checkpoint, routed CE ≈ dense). Its low per-step cosine (≈0.40) is just the dense step's own noise (cos(D, E) ≈ 0.09). Random selection on the same checkpoint stays biased (0.18; outlier CE 0.39 vs 0.095), so the model adapts to its router specifically. (3) The likely source of the gap between the 11–20× attribution bound and the 2–4× FLOPs-to-match is the early, off-distribution phase, where the routed update is biased (8–12× norm, mostly orthogonal to dense), plus the block-7 stage (expected cosine 0.87, the binding constraint); a block-16-only 7% split with the router stays at 0.983 / 0.944 on the co-adapted model. Untested: the early phase directly, and a late-split-only training run. eval_size 512 rows per checkpoint (32 steps of 16); Adam second moment estimated from 32 dense batches; router = ridge emulation (Spearman 0.54–0.64 to its target); embedding/LM head excluded.     Per-token, per-layer gradient: what it looks like

    Answer and query tokens carry most of the gradient; gold documents light up through every layer while distractors fade above layer ~18; ranking at block 7 is a poor ranking for the top half, which the recipe's block-16 re-selection already handles.

    What is plotted

    Color scale

It is almost all in blocks 0–15, with isolated tokens above 16, and inside the gold document it is a few tokens (the answer-bearing span).
    • Depth. The 3–4 orders-of-magnitude decay of document ||dL/dh|| that looks gentle on log means that, in absolute terms, nearly all of it is in blocks 0–7.

    What the maps show

    • Answer and query tokens dominate. 2–10% of tokens, 60–95% of blocks 7–31 energy on trained checkpoints (outlier at dense 1,952 is the exception, 30%).
    • Gold documents vs distractors. Per document token, gold carries 45–55× the distractor energy on nq (dense), 8–29× on contradiction, but only 2.4–3.8× on outlier, where the task is to read every document. That matches the oracle result that any scheme leaving documents empty breaks outlier. The per-row and global oracle sets sit almost entirely inside the gold documents; the router's per-document keep set spreads over every document by design.
    • A layer step at ~17–20. Training moves gradient mass to the top (block 31 alone holds 18–48% at dense 7,808), and on trained checkpoints distractor energy drops a further 1–2 orders of magnitude around layers 17–20, a dark band visible in every 2k map. The base model has no such step.
    • Gradients shrink as rows get solved. Document-token ||dL/dh|| at layer 7 is ≈2 orders of magnitude below base at 1,952 rows/task and ≈3 at 7,808; what is left sits on the answer (answer share 0.46–0.77 at 7,808 vs 0.15–0.39 at base).
    • The routed forward barely changes the picture. On the recipe checkpoint, routed answer CE is close to dense and the layer and token-type profile hardly move; kept document tokens' ||dL/dh|| rises 1.6–4× above block 7, and in blocks 0–6 the gradient shifts toward kept (often gold) documents. Per-row routed/dense energy ratios can be huge (one nq row 270×), but only on rows dense had nearly solved; per 16-row batch the median is ≈1.4× in norm units, consistent with the gradient-gap probe's 0.89 averaged over steps.
    • Cells vs whole tokens. 95% of a row's blocks 7–31 energy needs 2.5–3× fewer token×layer cells than whole tokens (nq 1.5% vs 4.2%, oolong 12% vs 29% at dense 1,952). Token ranks agree between adjacent layers (0.93) but only 0.33–0.39 between block 7 and block 31: two blocks, ≈0–15 and ≈17–31. That argues for re-selecting once per half, as the recipe's block-16 stage does (a third stage would belong at the layer-17–20 step), not for per-layer cells. These are attribution shares: the frozen oracle showed attribution concentration does not carry over once tokens are removed from the forward, and finer cells remove context more finely.
